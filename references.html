4-2-1 and adult maintenance

4-2-1: first 10 kg × 4, next 10 kg × 2, and each kg over 20 × 1 mL/hr. Multiply the rate by 24 for mL/day. At 70 kg: 110 mL/hr and 2,640 mL/day. At 100 kg: 140 mL/hr and 3,360 mL/day.

This is a historical hourly estimate. It does not prescribe a bolus or replace a fasting deficit.

NICE CG174 §1.4: routine adult water needs start at 25–30 mL/kg/day. Consider 20–25 for older or frail adults, kidney or heart failure, or malnutrition with refeeding risk. Use ideal weight and the lower range for obesity. Routine needs rarely exceed 3 L/day; seek expert help if BMI exceeds 40. Count other fluid intake and reassess.

Pregnancy, severe organ disease, diabetes, burns, and brain injury need a specialist plan. This ward guidance is not a universal surgical or ICU prescription.

The app compares water volumes only. Fluid type, salts, glucose, losses, and clinical response need separate review.

NICE CG174 §§1.1, 1.2, 1.4 · Holliday–Segar, 1957: historical basis

EXPAREL — labeled adult uses

Strength: 13.3 mg/mL. Vials: 133 mg/10 mL or 266 mg/20 mL. Do not use 13 mg/mL from the rounded “1.3%” name. Do not convert plain bupivacaine doses to EXPAREL.

Infiltration: up to 266 mg, chosen for the site and patient. Interscalene and popliteal sciatic blocks: 133 mg. Adductor canal: 133 mg/10 mL plus 50 mg/10 mL of 0.5% bupivacaine HCl, mixed to 20 mL. This app does not derive doses for other blocks or multiple sites.

One administration only. Obstetric paracervical block is contraindicated. Pregnancy and unlisted routes are not recommended by the label. This app stops those plans.

US prescribing information §§1–5, 7. PI revised 11/2023; DailyMed record updated 2025-12-12, checked 2026-10-21.

Mixing, timing, and toxicity

Bupivacaine HCl may be mixed with or given right before EXPAREL under the label’s 1:2 mg ratio. This app uses listed HCl mg ≤ half the listed EXPAREL mg. This is a conservative app check; manufacturer salt-equivalence examples differ. Passing the check does not prove safety.

Do not mix with lidocaine or other local anesthetics. Wait at least 20 minutes after local lidocaine before EXPAREL. There is no verified interval here for another drug. Avoid more local anesthetic for 96 hours after EXPAREL. Labeled companion analgesia still needs a clinician’s plan.

Using these medicines together increases toxicity risk. Trained staff, monitoring, and rescue equipment must be ready.

Manufacturer PI §§2.3–2.5, 5.2, 7 · Manufacturer dosing interpretation

Dilution and preparation

Use preservative-free 0.9% sodium chloride or lactated Ringer’s. Never use water or other hypotonic fluids. The label gives 0.89 mg/mL and a rounded 1:14 volume dilution. This app uses an exact 0.89 floor, counting HCl in the same syringe. It can reject rounded manufacturer examples; pharmacist review is required.

Gently invert before drawing up. Check the vial for damage, particles, or discoloration. Do not use it if frozen or exposed above 40°C (104°F) for a long period. Do not heat, autoclave, or filter. Use a 25-gauge or larger-bore needle. Inject slowly and aspirate often. Keep wet antiseptic away from the drug. Use diluted syringes within 4 hours. Discard unused drug.

Preparation: PI §§2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 16 · Manufacturer volume examples

Other local anesthetics

For each drug: percent × 10 = mg/mL; volume × mg/mL = mg. Same-drug rows are added. The lowest selected limit is used. Using different local anesthetics together can raise toxicity risk. This app stops different-drug plans, except the separately checked EXPAREL/bupivacaine HCl plan. It does not calculate a shared “safe dose” fraction.

These are selected product and route references for adults. Use the lowest effective dose. Check the actual product, site, timing, allergies, other medicines, and health conditions. No repeat-dose or infusion clearance is provided.

Lidocaine HCl

Healthy adult individual-dose reference: plain, lower of 4.5 mg/kg or 300 mg; with epinephrine, lower of 7 mg/kg or 500 mg. This app supports infiltration and peripheral blocks only. Other procedures can have different limits. Older or ill patients may need less.

Label §§2.2, 2.6, 5
Bupivacaine HCl

Label guide: infiltration 0.25%; peripheral block 0.25% or 0.5%. Clinician-selected limit must stay within 175 mg plain or 225 mg with 1:200,000 epinephrine. These are average-adult guide ceilings, not universal doses. Label repeat-dose guidance and 400 mg/24-hour limit do not authorize an automatic repeat.

Label §§2.2–2.6
Mepivacaine HCl

Choose a limit no higher than 400 mg per procedure for a healthy adult. Infiltration: 0.5% or 1%; peripheral block: 1% or 2%. The 0.5% product requires approved dilution. Do not infer permission for a mixture with another local anesthetic. The label’s 1,000 mg/24-hour limit does not authorize repeat dosing.

Polocaine PI: warnings and dose table
Ropivacaine

Infiltration guide: 0.2% or 0.5%, up to 200 mg. Major nerve block: 0.5% up to 250 mg; 0.75% up to 300 mg. A clinician must select the limit. These upper guide values are not universal maxima. Minor or field blocks do not use the major-block guide. A 300 mg brachial plexus dose may approach brain toxicity levels.

Label §§2.2, 5.7
Chloroprocaine

Plain: lower of 11 mg/kg or 800 mg. With 1:200,000 epinephrine: lower of 14 mg/kg or 1,000 mg. This app uses the 2%/3% infiltration and nerve-block product. Do not transfer these limits to a spinal product. Procedure guides and patient factors may call for less.

Retained adult tools

ABL, body-size estimates, adult burn formulas, and the LAST reference remain review tools. Their source matrix and remaining limits are in review notes. No result selects a transfusion threshold or replaces bedside care.

Simple blood-loss estimate

Estimated blood volume × (starting lab − selected minimum) ÷ starting lab. The model can underestimate loss as blood becomes diluted. The clinician must choose the blood-volume factor and minimum. No transfusion decision follows from this result.

Body-size estimates

IBW is Devine’s height-based estimate; LBW uses the Janmahasatian adult equation; BSA uses Mosteller. PBW uses the ARDSNet ventilation-size equation. These values do not select a drug dose.

Adult burns

ABA: start from 2 mL/kg/% for adults with burns covering at least 20% of body surface. Historical Parkland uses 4. Formula volumes require bedside adjustment. The clock starts at injury. There is no automatic catch-up dose.
