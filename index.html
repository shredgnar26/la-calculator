Which patient factors matter?

Hepatic or cardiac disease, pregnancy, renal dysfunction, acidosis, frailty and extremes of age may change the plan. No fixed percentage reduction is assumed. Enter a lower clinician-selected limit where applicable.

What about blood loss?

Blood-loss replacement is not included above. It is a separate need in addition to maintenance.

Notify the anesthesia clinician as bleeding occurs. Do not wait for the next hour. Follow their replacement plan, accounting for fluids already given.

Fluid types explained

Crystalloid: fluids such as lactated Ringer’s replace fluid volume.

Albumin (a colloid): expands blood volume; it does not replace red cells or clotting factors.

Blood products: replace red cells or support clotting, depending on the product.

Synthetic starch colloids are not interchangeable with albumin; they carry FDA warnings about kidney injury, bleeding and mortality.

Formula estimate, not a resuscitation order. Actual fluids follow patient response and the anesthesia clinician’s plan.

References and assumptions

Deficit = maintenance × fasting hours, allocated 50% / 25% / 25%. Partial hours use a uniform rate within each hour. No automatic blood-loss or third-space replacement is added.

Compare hemoglobin and hematocrit

Both use: estimated blood volume × (starting value − selected minimum) ÷ starting value.

With 5,250 mL estimated blood volume, 14→10 g/dL and 42→30% each give 1,500 mL because the proportional change is identical.

Enter measured values in their original units. Hematocrit is not automatically set to three times hemoglobin. Switching modes preserves each mode’s entries.

Burn fluids

Select the clinical protocol with the burn team.

Use an age-appropriate Lund–Browder assessment. Pediatric maintenance and glucose requirements need the burn team’s plan; no adult default is applied.

Count partial- and full-thickness burns, not superficial redness. Enter the assessed percentage; the former fixed pediatric chart has been removed.

The clock starts at injury

Review elapsed time and fluids already received before setting a rate. Reassess with the burn team; do not give an automatic catch-up bolus to meet a formula.

20% lipid emulsion

If the patient remains unstable

Repeat the bolus and double the infusion per the checklist. Do not apply an automatic five-minute waiting rule. Account for all lipid already given.

After stabilization

Continue lipid for more than 15 minutes once hemodynamically stable. Observe for 2 hours after seizure, 4–6 hours after cardiovascular instability, and as appropriate after cardiac arrest.

Recognition and resuscitation reminders

Symptoms can include mouth numbness, metallic taste, ringing in the ears, altered behavior, seizures, arrhythmia or collapse. Early symptoms may be absent, and onset may be delayed.

LAST resuscitation differs from standard ACLS. Ensure adequate oxygenation and ventilation; avoid hypoxia and acidosis. Benzodiazepines are preferred for seizures. Use the official checklist for epinephrine and medication-avoidance instructions. Propofol is not a substitute for lipid emulsion.
