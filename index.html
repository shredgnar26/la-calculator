EXPAREL checks — required

One dose and one site only. No epidural, spinal, IV, joint, or other block calculations.

The 20-minute rule applies to lidocaine given before EXPAREL. It does not allow mixing or later lidocaine. Record that earlier dose and review its toxicity risk separately.

Use only the two listed dilution fluids. Water and other low-salt fluids can damage EXPAREL.

Compare with NICE adult maintenance

Choose the weight for this reference. This does not change the 4-2-1 weight.

For obesity, NICE uses ideal weight and the lower range. Adults rarely need over 3 L/day for routine maintenance. Get expert help when BMI is over 40.

Older or frail adults, kidney or heart failure, and risk of refeeding may need less. Count oral, tube, IV drug, and other fluid intake. Review fluid balance, labs, and response.

Specialist conditions such as pregnancy, severe organ disease, diabetes, burns, or brain injury need a separate plan.

These water ranges do not choose fluid type, salts, or glucose. NICE CG174 is a hospital maintenance guide; it is not a surgical replacement formula.

An estimate, not a transfusion trigger

Use patient condition, bleeding rate, laboratory results, and the clinician’s plan. ABL is not converted to blood-product units.

Compare hemoglobin and hematocrit

Both use a simple model: estimated blood volume × (starting value − selected minimum) ÷ starting value.

With 5,250 mL estimated blood volume, 14→10 g/dL and 42→30% each give 1,500 mL because the proportional change is identical.

Enter measured values in their original units. Hematocrit is not automatically set to three times hemoglobin. Switching modes preserves each mode’s entries.

Method and references

This model treats lost blood as having the starting blood count. Dilution can make it underestimate loss. Other methods give different results. Do not use this number as a transfusion trigger.

20% lipid emulsion

If the patient remains unstable

Repeat the bolus and double the infusion per the checklist. Do not apply an automatic five-minute waiting rule. Account for all lipid already given.

After stabilization

Continue lipid for more than 15 minutes once hemodynamically stable. Observe for 2 hours after seizure, 4–6 hours after cardiovascular instability, and as appropriate after cardiac arrest.

Recognition and resuscitation reminders

Symptoms can include mouth numbness, metallic taste, ringing in the ears, altered behavior, seizures, arrhythmia or collapse. Early symptoms may be absent, and onset may be delayed.

LAST resuscitation differs from standard ACLS. Ensure adequate oxygenation and ventilation; avoid hypoxia and acidosis. Benzodiazepines are preferred for seizures. Use the official checklist for epinephrine and medication-avoidance instructions. Propofol is not a substitute for lipid emulsion.
